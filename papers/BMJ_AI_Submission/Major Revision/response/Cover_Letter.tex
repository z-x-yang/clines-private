\noindent Dear Dr.\ Paton,

\bigskip

We are pleased to resubmit our manuscript, ``\textbf{CLINES: Clinical LLM-based Information Extraction and Structuring Agent}'' (manuscript ID \texttt{bmjdh-2026-000027}), revised in response to the recent Major Revision decision. We thank the editorial office and the five reviewers for the depth and rigor of the feedback, which has materially strengthened the manuscript.

\medskip

The revised manuscript incorporates substantial additions to address the reviewers' major concerns. Specifically, we have:

\begin{itemize}
    \item conducted a structured \textbf{inter-annotator agreement (IAA)} study on six cross-annotated notes (three per dataset for 4CE and CORAL), reporting a pooled mention-level F1 of 0.82 and an assertion Cohen's $\kappa$ of 0.85 following pre-specified structural-disambiguation guidelines, and explicitly reframed MIMIC-III F1 as agreement-with-annotator (addressing R1.1, R2.2, R5.4.2);
    \item added \textbf{percentile bootstrap 95\% confidence intervals} ($B=2000$ document-level resamples) and paired-bootstrap, FDR-corrected pairwise significance tests to every primary F1 comparison (R1.2), and a document-level resampling stability analysis (R1.3);
    \item completed a \textbf{seven-class hallucination taxonomy} with stratified manual judging of 700 candidate false positives, finding that 54.3\% of apparent false positives are not true model errors but rather annotation gaps, with each true-hallucination category accounting for $\leq 17\%$ of cases (R1.9, R2.3, R4.3, R5.3.2);
    \item added \textbf{cost, latency, and computational efficiency} reporting per dataset and per model, including total LLM invocations per note and Azure OpenAI list-price USD per note (R1.5, R4.4);
    \item expanded the baseline cohort with \textbf{o3-mini single-prompt}, \textbf{GPT-4o chain-of-thought single-prompt}, and two off-the-shelf clinical deep-learning encoders (\textbf{BERT-base clinical NER} and \textbf{GatorTron-base}), closing the previous baseline asymmetry (R1.7, R3.8, R5.4.1);
    \item performed a \textbf{component-wise ablation} isolating the contribution of semantic chunking, SapBERT normalization, the date module, and cross-chunk reconciliation (R1.8, R3.5);
    \item replaced the STROBE reporting checklist with \textbf{MI-CLAIM} and \textbf{TRIPOD+AI} checklists in the supplementary materials (R1.4, R5.3.3);
    \item reframed all instances of ``agentic'' and ``zero-shot'' to more precise language (R1.6, R2.1), tempered scalability and interoperability claims with explicit discussion of FP8 precision for Llama-3.1-405B, GDPR considerations, and UMLS-to-SNOMED/LOINC/RxNorm/OMOP-CDM mapping requirements (R5.4.3, R5.4.4);
    \item added the BMJ DH\&AI ``Objective / Methods and Analysis / Results / Conclusion'' abstract structure plus the required summary box (R1.18, R5.2.1, R5.2.2);
    \item clarified annotator credentialing (all annotators completed CITI Human Subjects Research training) and explicitly stated that LLM inference used \textbf{HMS Information Technology's managed Azure AI service (HMS Azure AI)} on de-identified data only, with provider models neither retaining nor training on submitted text (R1.15);
    \item added a Patient and Public Involvement statement (R1.20), replaced the vague ethics statement with an accurate one---under the U.S. Common Rule (45~CFR~46.104) the secondary analysis of fully de-identified data does not constitute human-subjects research and required no IRB approval at the authors' institution (R1.21)---and corrected the i2b2 2010 citation to Uzuner et al.\ 2011 (R1.10);
    \item incorporated and discussed the three contemporary comparators highlighted by Reviewer 5 (John Snow Labs Healthcare NLP, CLEAR, and Ntinopoulos et al.\ 2025), with reasoned positioning where direct empirical comparison was not feasible (R5.4.5).
\end{itemize}
